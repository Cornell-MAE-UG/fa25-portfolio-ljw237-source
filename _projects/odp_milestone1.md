## Milestone 1 — Client Pitch

[← Back to ODP](/fa25-portfolio-ljw237-source/projects/odp/)

### Grapevine Redirection and Attraction for Pest Elimination

**Team:** Save the Grapes  
**Clients:** Cornell CALS Extension / E&J Gallo Winery / National Grape Association

---

### Problem Statement

Spotted lanternflies (SLFs) land on grape vines and feed on their sap, contaminating harvests and resulting in grapes that taste worse for consumers. SLFs also release mold, which covers leaves and harms vine growth. A Penn State study found an average of 22.9 lanternflies on a single grape vine. When a load of juice grapes can be rejected if 1-2 adult SLFs are found in a 1,000g sample, sufficiently thorough on-plant removal methods risk damaging vines — making attraction-based diversion a more viable approach.

### Impact

Grape farmers face significant losses if their harvests are damaged or destroyed. When SLFs land on grape vines and suck their sap, plant growth is hindered and grapes become sour. Grapes grown in regions no longer affected by spotted lanternflies would taste sweeter, farmers and consumers would not have to fear contamination, and the price of grape products would remain stable due to increased production volume.

![Client Pitch Figure]({{ "/assets/images/odp_m1_figure.png" | relative_url }})

### Concept A: False Grape Vine

Multiple vines would be set up throughout the vineyard with the discretion of the owner. These fake vines would be full of Tree of Heaven sap which SLFs prefer, as well as a 60Hz emitter which SLFs are attracted to. Lanternflies would land and take the sap from the fake grape vine instead of real grape vines.

### Improving the Current Status Quo

Farms currently spray pesticides to deter SLFs, which fade after only a few days. Our solution involves no interference with existing growing processes — the traps do not touch the grape vines.

### End-of-Semester Proof of Concept

One single vine made from 3D prints, wood, and purchased parts, with a 60Hz emitter and liquid to simulate Tree of Heaven sap.

### Key Risks

- **Risk 1** — Faux grape vine traps may occupy usable growing space in vineyards. We will test whether trap size affects effectiveness by altering dimensions.
- **Risk 2** — The 60Hz emitter may attract other insects or wildlife unintentionally.
- **Risk 3** — Tree of Heaven sap availability and artificial replication may be difficult to source at scale.
